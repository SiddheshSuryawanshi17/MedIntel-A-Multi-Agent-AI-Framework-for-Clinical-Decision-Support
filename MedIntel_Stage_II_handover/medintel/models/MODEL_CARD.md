# Model card: risk-lr-uci 1.0.0 (36286f9d)

Generated by `uv run medintel train`. Research prototype on public de-identified data; not a medical device.

## Intended use

Estimates the probability of angiographic coronary artery disease (over 50% diameter narrowing) in adults referred for evaluation, as one of four inputs to MedIntel's fusion step (Equation 4.2). The rules, the evidence and a clinician's review always come on top of it. The training population was referred for coronary angiography, so its disease rate (46%) is far higher than in a general clinic; probabilities will run high for routine check-ups.

## Data

- Janosi, A., Steinbrunn, W., Pfisterer, M. and Detrano, R. (1989). Heart Disease [Dataset]. UCI Machine Learning Repository. https://doi.org/10.24432/C52P4X (CC BY 4.0).
- Split: stratified 60/20/20 on the Cleveland data, seed 2026: 181 train, 61 validation, 61 test.
- A value that was not recorded is set to the training average, contributes nothing to the score, and is listed as filled in on the report.

## Two submodels

- **Pre-test model** (7 inputs): age, sex, chest pain type, resting systolic BP, total cholesterol, fasting glucose above 120 mg/dL, resting ECG. Used when no exercise test is on record, which covers routine check-up records.
- **Full model** (13 inputs): the above plus peak exercise heart rate, exercise-induced angina, exercise ST depression, peak exercise ST slope, major vessels on fluoroscopy and the thallium stress test. Used when any of peak heart rate, exercise angina or ST depression is recorded.

Why two: filling in many correlated inputs with averages distorts the rest. In the full model, age carries a small negative weight once peak heart rate is known; with peak heart rate filled in, older patients would look safer. A separate pre-test model learns the plain effect of each input instead.

Both are logistic regressions (L2) on centered inputs (numeric inputs also scaled), with Platt scaling fitted on out-of-fold scores of the training split. Each feature's term is its exact SHAP value.

## Limitations

- Small single-site development data (303 Cleveland Clinic patients, donated to UCI in 1988); the test split has 61 patients.
- The work-up inputs (fluoroscopy, thallium test) are only available after specialist testing.
- Not validated for patients under 18, or outside a referral population.
- In this referral data, patients without chest pain (asymptomatic) had the highest disease rate and typical angina a lower one, so the model gives typical angina a negative contribution. That reflects who was referred for angiography, not what the symptom means clinically; read the contributions with that in mind.
- Sex is recorded as male or female only, as in the source data.
